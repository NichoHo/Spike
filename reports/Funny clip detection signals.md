# Spike should listen for laughter first

Without chat, the best signals for funny moments are laughter in the audio, the transcript read by an LLM, and crowd replay data where it exists. Spike should add all three to its upload path instead of letting the LLM find moments alone. Research is consistent on modalities. Text carries most of the humor signal: on UR-FUNNY, text alone reached 62.85% accuracy, and adding audio and video only lifted that to 64.47%. Laughter is the standard free label for "this was funny", used by sitcom, TED, and stand-up datasets. LLMs are weak humor judges, though. They match humans on only 43 to 51% of stand-up punchlines and correlate with human funniness ratings at roughly 0.2. So the LLM should rank and trim candidates that audio and crowd signals propose, not sweep a whole video unaided. On YouTube, the "Most Replayed" heatmap (readable through yt-dlp, which Spike already uses) and timestamped comments are free crowd signals that partly replace chat. A CHI 2025 study found heatmap-built highlights comparable to human-made ones. For a $0 single-PC build, the main changes to BLUEPRINT section 6 are these. Add a CPU laughter/shout tagger (about a minute of compute per audio hour, inferred). Add `replay_heat` and `comment_heat` signals. Run signal peak-picking on uploads too. Put the two scores on one scale before the 0.7/0.3 blend. Re-rank the top few pairwise. Anchor clip boundaries on the setup before the laugh. Use Gemini's free-tier audio-visual judging only on short windows, mainly for public YouTube URLs.

## Laughter is the audio signal that means "funny", loudness only means "loud"

Spike's current `audio_loud` signal (ffmpeg `ebur128` LUFS) fires on shouting, game explosions, and music as readily as on jokes. Vendors that relied on loudness admit its limits. Powder's "Universal Game Support" listened for "shouts and volume spikes as a rough stand-in for a kill feed" ([GameClipper Advanced](https://gameclipperadvanced.com/blog/powder-gg-alternatives/)). Eklipse calls its old engine, "trigger event (kill feed or audio spike) + fixed 15 to 30 s window", one that "falls apart" outside clean kill-feed games ([Eklipse blog](https://blog.eklipse.gg/streaming-tips/how-gameplay-intelligence-works-2.html)). In academic benchmarks, generic audio is the weakest single modality. On the UMT ablation, audio alone scored **13.73 mAP on QVHighlights versus 37.79 for visual**, and adding audio to visual gained only about 1 to 1.5 points ([UMT, arXiv 2203.12745](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.12745)).

Laughter is different, because it is an outcome signal and not a content feature. Humor datasets use it as ground truth. UR-FUNNY labels TED punchlines by audience laughter markup ([arXiv 1904.06618](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.06618v1)). MHD uses the Big Bang Theory laugh track ([WACV 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Patro_Multimodal_Humor_Dataset_Predicting_Laughter_Tracks_for_Sitcoms_WACV_2021_paper.pdf)). StandUp4AI auto-labeled **128,194 laughter instances across 334 hours** of stand-up ([arXiv 2505.18903](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18903)). FunnyNet-W treats the **8 seconds before laughter onset** as the funny clip and claims state of the art on five datasets. Its laughter detector reached 78.4% temporal precision on Friends ([arXiv 2401.04210](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.04210v1)). The caveat is that all of this evidence is audience or laugh-track laughter. No study measures a solo streamer's own laugh as a highlight predictor, so that link is plausible but unproven.

Cheap detectors exist. **YAMNet** has 3.7M weights and does 69.2M multiplies per 0.96 s frame. Its 521 AudioSet classes include Laughter, Giggle, Snicker, Belly laugh, Chuckle, Shout, Yell, Whoop, and Screaming ([TF models README](https://github.com/tensorflow/models/blob/master/research/audioset/yamnet/README.md); [TF tutorial](https://www.tensorflow.org/tutorials/audio/transfer_learning_audio)). That works out to roughly 260 GMAC per audio hour, which should be about a minute of CPU (an inference, not a benchmark). One catch: it needs Keras 2. **PANNs CNN14** is heavier but more accurate (0.431 AudioSet mAP). It ran 10 s of audio in 390.8 ms on a Raspberry Pi 5 ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/litert-community/PANNs-CNN14-AudioSet-LiteRT)). Dedicated laughter models exist too: jrgillick's MIT-licensed detector, stale since 2021 ([GitHub](https://github.com/jrgillick/laughter-detection)), and omine's 2024 segmenter, whose weights are research-use only ([GitHub](https://github.com/omine-me/LaughterSegmentation)). Whisper-AT tags AudioSet classes for under 1% extra compute ([arXiv 2307.03183](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.03183)). However, it hooks the openai-whisper encoder, not faster-whisper, so for Spike it would mean a second encoder pass ([GitHub](https://github.com/YuanGongND/whisper-at)). None of these have published accuracy on game-plus-voice stream mixes. Expect false positives from meme sounds and alerts with canned laughs.

Two tempting shortcuts fail. Whisper's own "[laughter]" tags are sporadic and not reproducible. One user ran the same 2-minute stand-up clip hundreds of times and got an applause tag once ([OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/speech-to-text-whisper-1-detection-of-laughter-applause-cheers/1371933)). Laughter also gets merged into neighboring word timestamps ([StandUp4AI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.18903)). That corruption is a free weak cue, though: a word with an abnormally long duration often hides a laugh (inferred). Prosody helps only when fused with text. FunnyNet-W's audio plus text scored **84.5 F1 versus 77.8 for text alone** ([arXiv 2401.04210](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.04210v1)), while the classic "pause before the punchline" cue is contested ([Pickering 2011](https://faculty.tamuc.edu/lpickering/Pdfs/Publish_11.pdf)). Heavier audio tools do not pay off on a whole VOD. Demucs voice separation runs at roughly 0.2x to 1.5x real time on CPU ([StemSplit](https://huggingface.co/StemSplitio/htdemucs-ft-vocals-onnx); [guide](https://tomodahinata.com/en/blog/demucs-v4-music-source-separation-production-guide)). pyannote 3.1 diarization took 36.5 minutes for a 45-minute file on 8 vCPUs ([pyannote #1626](https://github.com/pyannote/pyannote-audio/issues/1626)). Speech emotion models like emotion2vec+ are cheap ([HF](https://huggingface.co/emotion2vec/emotion2vec_plus_base)), but nothing links their output to clip-worthiness.

## Transcripts hold most of the humor, but LLMs grade it poorly

Text is the strongest single modality in nearly every humor ablation. On UR-FUNNY, text alone gave 62.85% accuracy, audio plus video only 53.3%, and all three 64.47%, against a human ceiling of 82.5% ([arXiv 1904.06618](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.06618v1)). On Friends, FunnyNet-W's text branch beat both audio and visual branches. Its Whisper transcripts matched ground-truth subtitles within a fraction of a point (TBBT 78.2 vs 78.5 F1) ([arXiv 2401.04210](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.04210v1)). Spike's faster-whisper transcript is therefore good enough input. The SVHighlights benchmark, built on 640 hours of long sports broadcasts, agrees. Adding transcripts to visual captions gave **+6.25 HIT@1**, and audio volume added little beyond that ([arXiv 2606.06926](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06926v2)).

The weak link is the judge. When asked to extract punchlines from stand-up transcripts, GPT-4o matched ground truth 48.9% of the time and Claude 3.5 Sonnet 43.4%. Gemma models scored only 30 to 35%, and GPT-4o fell to 25.4% under semantic scoring. Persona prompts and humor-type prompts did not help: a plain "extract the key humorous lines and punchlines" worked best. Larger models also tended to explain the joke instead of quoting it ([arXiv 2504.09049](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09049)). On absolute funniness ratings, Spearman correlation with humans was **0.266 for Claude Sonnet 4, 0.224 for GPT-4.1, and 0.169 for Gemini 2.5 Pro** ([arXiv 2511.09133](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09133v1)). On HumorBench-Hard, no model exceeded 60%, and failures clustered on implicit leaps and obscure references ([arXiv 2507.21476](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21476v1)). Humans are noisy too. HumorRank's human validation had inter-rater agreement of only about 0.43 Krippendorff alpha ([Pith summary](https://pith.science/paper/2604.19786)). Any score has a low ceiling, and small local models sit at the bottom of every humor benchmark.

Two practical conclusions follow. First, the LLM should judge a short, pre-selected window with its setup included, not hunt across hours. The one open-source clipper that measured itself against human Twitch clips found its LLM agent reached **Recall@10 of 0.708 versus 0.917 for a tuned chat-rate baseline**, at $0.10 and 15.6 s per VOD versus near zero. The sample was tiny (4 VODs, 13 moments) ([cliptron](https://github.com/infectedresearch/cliptron)). Second, transcripts miss delivery, so the prompt should carry the non-verbal events the audio pass found. Writing "[laughter 01:02:05]" or "[shouting]" into the transcript lines is an inference from the CMCL authors' complaint that transcripts lack tone and audience reaction, not a tested technique.

## Video adds little on its own, except through Gemini, which both sees and hears

Cheap visual signals are weak for humor. MediaPipe Face Landmarker outputs 52 blendshapes, including `jawOpen` and `mouthSmileLeft/Right` ([Google](https://ai.google.dev/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/face_landmarker)). HSEmotion's fast FER models reach only **60 to 65% accuracy on AffectNet** ([HSEmotion](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/364625072_HSEmotion_High-speed_emotion_recognition_library)), and live-stream lighting and backgrounds make it harder ([PMC9399731](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9399731/)). No source measures facecam smiles as a highlight predictor. PySceneDetect finds cuts, not highlights ([docs](https://www.scenedetect.com/docs/latest/api/detectors.html)). Stream VODs have almost no cuts, so cut density is useless there, though cuts still help snap boundaries on edited uploads. CLIP-style frame scoring fails "when frames look alike and only audio disambiguates" ([arXiv 2609.14790](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14790)), which describes most talking-head and static-gameplay footage. Game-event detection (Medal's game integrations, kill-feed OCR) finds hype, not humor, and needs per-game work ([Medal](https://medal.tv/auto-clipping); [GameClipper Advanced](https://gameclipperadvanced.com/blog/powder-gg-alternatives/)). MUStARD offers a warning: its visual gains in the speaker-dependent setting partly reflected character bias, not real cues ([arXiv 1906.01815](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.01815)).

Multimodal video LLMs are the exception, because they catch visual gags and delivery that a transcript cannot. **Gemini is the only major API that ingests video with its audio and accepts public YouTube URLs directly.** It samples 1 fps plus 1 Kbps mono audio with per-second timestamps. The free tier allows **8 hours of YouTube video per day**. `start_offset`/`end_offset` lets you send only a window, and custom `fps` raises sampling for fast gags ([Gemini video docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding)). Google itself warns that 1 fps sampling loses fast action. Token rates are 66 per frame at low resolution, 258 at standard, and 32 per second of audio. At current prices, a full hour at low resolution costs about **$0.06 on 2.5 Flash-Lite**, $0.12 on 3.1 Flash-Lite, and about $1.44 on 3.1 Pro ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing); arithmetic from the token rates). Judging 30 one-minute candidate windows at 1 fps low resolution costs about $0.03 per VOD on 2.5 Flash-Lite, so the free tier covers it. Gemini 2.5 Pro "rivals specialized fine-tuned models" on QVHighlights moment retrieval ([Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/gemini-2-5-video-understanding/)), but no measured hallucination rate exists for "find the funniest moments" prompts over hour-long inputs. Claude takes frames, not video. Local Qwen3-VL-8B runs in llama.cpp at Q4 in about 6 to 7 GB of VRAM. Its grounding is coarse (Charades-STA R@0.3 of 69.3), and CPU-only use is impractical beyond a handful of windows ([Qwen GGUF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct-GGUF); [REZE](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04480v1)).

## The crowd already marked the replays on YouTube

The YouTube "Most Replayed" heatmap is the closest no-chat equivalent to Twitch community clips. yt-dlp exposes it as `heatmap`, a list of `start_time`, `end_time`, and a `value` normalized from 0 to 1 ([yt-dlp common.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/master/yt_dlp/extractor/common.py)). It always has **100 equal bins**, so a 3-hour VOD gets about 108 s bins ([yt-dlp tests](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/master/yt_dlp/extractor/youtube/_video.py)). The academic backing is real. Mr. HiSum built a **31,892-video** highlight benchmark using Most Replayed as labels, aggregated over 50,000+ users per video ([GitHub](https://github.com/MRHiSum/MR.HiSum)). A CHI 2025 study found heatmap-generated highlights "indistinguishable from those created by humans in viewing experience" ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713880)). There are limits. The heatmap marks replays and seeks, not laughs. A 2026 study needed a position control because of a strong start-of-video effect, and found nothing beat simple loudness/motion baselines at predicting it ([arXiv 2607.01400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01400)). It also appears only after enough viewing, sits outside the official API, and can break when YouTube changes its player.

Timestamped comments fill the "why" gap. A comment like "2:34 lmao" pins a second and labels it funny. The official Data API `commentThreads.list` costs 1 quota unit per call of up to 100 threads ([YouTube Data API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/commentThreads/list)). Both crowd signals exist only for videos that are already published and watched. They help with back-catalogs and other creators' uploads, not with fresh uploads.

Commercial and open-source clippers confirm that nobody has a secret detector. OpusClip's ClipAnything claims "visual, audio, and sentiment cues" and a virality score, with no method disclosed ([OpusClip help](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/9947095-clip-anything)). Reviewers report that the score ranks clips within a video but does not predict views ([ssemble](https://www.ssemble.com/blog/opus-clip-review-2026)). Users complain that it cuts jokes before the punchline and drops the context sentence ([eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/opusclip-reviews)). Eklipse lists game events, "shouts, laughs, and fast commentary", and on-screen actions ([Eklipse help](https://eklipse.gg/help/how-does-the-ai-actually-detect-a-highlight-in-my-stream/)). StreamLadder adds chat spikes and emotes ([StreamLadder](https://www.streamladder.com/blog/twitch-auto-clips-your-guide-to-automatically-capturing-stream-highlights)). The large open-source repos are all faster-whisper plus an LLM rubric returning score, hook, and reason, often with an Ollama or local option. They include openshorts (5,857 stars), AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator (5,191), and Alibaba's FunClip (6,359), and none publishes an evaluation ([openshorts](https://github.com/mutonby/openshorts); [AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator](https://github.com/Anil-matcha/AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator); [FunClip](https://github.com/modelscope/FunClip)). Spike's planned stack already exceeds them on signals. None of them fuses laughter detection, chat, community clips, and heatmap together.

## Fusion should be learned, scores normalized, and boundaries anchored on the setup

The fusion evidence is consistent: combining modalities beats any single one, but a weaker modality adds only a little. Chat added just **+0.8 to +2.5 F** on top of video in Fu et al.'s League of Legends study, where chat alone scored 43.2 F against 72.2 for video ([ACL 2017](https://aclanthology.org/D17-1102.pdf)). That study predates emote-aware chat and is about plays, not comedy, so it should not be read as a verdict on chat for funny streams. Hand weights are the right start, but weak labels let you replace them. LightGBM's `lambdarank` and `rank_xendcg` objectives take integer relevance labels grouped per query, and VOD is the natural query ([LightGBM docs](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Parameters.html)). Logistic regression on the same features is the lower-effort step before that.

The LLM score needs repair before blending. Pointwise LLM scores are poorly calibrated. Pairwise Ranking Prompting asks "which is better" in both orders to cancel position bias, and beat pointwise methods by over 10% on BEIR ([arXiv 2306.17563](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.17563)). Position bias is large. GPT-4 kept the same verdict after an order swap only 65% of the time ([MT-Bench](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.05685)). Scoring both orders and averaging 3 explain-then-score samples raised judge accuracy from 44.4% to 58.7% ([arXiv 2305.17926](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.17926)). G-Eval's probability-weighted score avoids integer ties ([arXiv 2303.16634](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.16634)), though the size of its gain did not reproduce cleanly in the notes. Bradley-Terry aggregation of pairwise humor judgments gave rankings that agreed across two LLM judges at Kendall tau 0.889 ([Pith summary](https://pith.science/paper/2604.19786)). There is also a plain scale bug in section 6.4. A 0 to 100 LLM rubric score is blended with a z-score "mapped to 0 to 100" whose spread is undefined, so the 0.7/0.3 split does not mean what it says. Converting both to per-VOD percentiles fixes that.

Boundaries matter as much as detection. The main complaint about commercial clippers, cutting before the punchline, is a boundary failure ([eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/opusclip-reviews)). The research pattern is setup before reaction. FunnyNet labels the seconds before laughter. UR-FUNNY pairs each punchline with its preceding context sentences. StandUp4AI tags the word right before the laugh ([arXiv 2401.04210](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.04210v1); [arXiv 1904.06618](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.06618v1); [arXiv 2505.18903](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18903)). SVHighlights got its best long-video results by merging shots into transcript-coherent segments before scoring ([arXiv 2606.06926](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06926v2)), which validates Spike's candidate-window design. Video LLM timestamps are too coarse to trust for edges. Whisper word times around laughter are corrupted, so the end edge should come from the laughter detector's offset, not from the last word.

Evaluation can borrow benchmark metrics directly. Use HIT@1 (is the top pick good), precision@k, and NDCG@k on graded labels ([QVHighlights](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.09609); [LightGBM docs](https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Parameters.html)), plus recall of known community clips, as cliptron did. Humor labels are noisy, so differences of a point or two on 15 VODs are probably noise. Spend labeling effort where the LLM and signal scores disagree. Routing the most uncertain 20% to a human closed most of the gap to human accuracy in Wang et al. ([arXiv 2305.17926](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.17926)).

## Recommended detection pipeline for Spike, and what to change in section 6

The pipeline keeps Spike's shape (cheap signals propose, the LLM judges) and extends it to uploads. Costs are for a 3-hour VOD on the minimum CPU PC. Figures marked "inferred" are estimates that Phase 0 must measure.

| Stage | What runs | Applies to | Added cost, 3 h VOD |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Per-second signals | Existing `chat_rate`, `chat_hype`, `clip_heat`, `audio_loud`, `speech_rate`, plus new `laugh`, `shout`, `replay_heat`, `comment_heat` | All; missing signals weight 0 | YAMNet about 3 min CPU (inferred); heatmap one yt-dlp metadata call; comments a few API units |
| 2. Candidates | Signal peaks (all sources) unioned with LLM transcript-chunk proposals (uploads) and an optional Gemini full-video proposal pass (public YouTube URLs) | All | Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite low res about $0.18 paid or free tier (8 h/day) |
| 3. Text judge | One LLM call per candidate, transcript with 30 to 60 s of setup and inline `[laughter]` / `[shouting]` / `[replayed]` markers, exact-quote punchline, start/end as line indices | All | Unchanged (about 30 calls) |
| 4. AV judge (optional) | Gemini on the top 2x `target_clip_count` windows at 1 to 2 fps low res, via URL plus offsets or an uploaded low-res section | Default on for public YouTube URLs, off for Twitch until eval shows lift | About $0.03 per VOD or free tier |
| 5. Fuse | Per-VOD percentiles of LLM score and signal score, then the weighted blend | All | Negligible |
| 6. Pairwise re-rank | Top 6 by fused score, both orders, win counts (15 pairs x 2 = 30 calls) | All | About 30 short LLM calls |
| 7. Boundaries | Start at a sentence start that includes the setup, end at laugh offset or next sentence end plus 0.6 s, then existing pause and scene-cut snapping | All | Negligible |

These are the specific edits to BLUEPRINT section 6.

| Section | Current design | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 6 intro, uploads path | "The LLM reads the transcript in chunks and proposes moments. Loudness breaks ties." | Uploads run the same signal peak-picking as streams. LLM chunk proposals become a second candidate source, unioned and deduped by IoU. The LLM is a weak sole finder (43 to 51% punchline agreement; cliptron LLM recall 0.708 vs 0.917 for a tuned signal baseline). |
| 6.1 signals | Five signals | Add `laugh`: per-second max YAMNet probability over Laughter, Giggle, Snicker, Belly laugh, and Chuckle. Count it only when the run lasts at least 0.5 s and transcript words fall within 3 s, to suppress canned meme laughs. Mark this gate `ponytail:` as a heuristic to tune. Add `shout` (Shout, Yell, Screaming, Whoop). Add `replay_heat`: yt-dlp `heatmap` values held flat across each bin, YouTube only, skipped when absent. Add `comment_heat`: timestamps parsed from top-level comments via the official Data API, weighted up when the comment matches a laugh lexicon, smoothed over 5 s. Optionally add `long_word`: words over about 1.5 s, a free laughter cue. |
| 6.1 fuse weights | One weight set | Start with two settings rows. With chat: chat_rate 0.25, chat_hype 0.20, clip_heat 0.20, laugh 0.15, audio_loud 0.10, shout 0.05, speech_rate 0.05. Without chat: laugh 0.30, replay_heat 0.25, comment_heat 0.15, audio_loud 0.15, shout 0.10, speech_rate 0.05. These are still guesses, and the rescaling rule for missing signals stays. |
| 6.2 peaks | Local maxima above z = 2.0, 60 s apart | Keep, but use `scipy.signal.find_peaks` with `distance` and `prominence` so plateaus do not yield duplicates. For laugh-anchored peaks, place the clip window before the laugh (`[p - 30, p + 8]`) rather than shifting the series, since setup precedes laughter. |
| 6.3 prompt | Transcript, chat excerpt, signal values | Inline non-verbal events into transcript lines. Ask for the exact quoted punchline line. Ask for `start_line`/`end_line` indices instead of free seconds and map them to word timestamps in code. Use a plain extraction instruction, not a persona. Average 2 samples, or use a logprob-weighted score when the backend exposes logprobs. Warn in settings that the local 7B fallback is the weakest humor judge. |
| 6.4 step 5 | `final = 0.7 * llm + 0.3 * signal` on mismatched scales | Convert both to per-VOD percentiles first. Keep 0.7/0.3 for uploads without crowd signals. Start chat-backed streams at 0.5/0.5, because the only measured comparison favored the chat baseline. Let Phase 7 eval decide. |
| 6.4 new step | None | After the overlap removal, re-rank the top 6 pairwise ("which clip would more viewers find funny?"), querying both orders and ordering by wins. |
| 6.4 step 3 | Snap end to current word plus 0.6 s | When a laugh run overlaps the end, end at laugh offset plus 0.6 s, because Whisper word times inside laughter are unreliable. |
| 6 new subsection: learning and eval | Phase 7 "replaces weights" | Log every candidate's per-signal max and mean, LLM score, and outcome to `candidates`. Labels are review accept/reject, posted, `post_stats` normalized per channel, community-clip overlap, and heatmap overlap. Fit logistic regression once a few hundred labeled windows exist (an unsourced estimate), then LightGBM `rank_xendcg` grouped by VOD. Freeze 5 to 10 VODs as a test set. Report HIT@1, P@5, NDCG@5, and community-clip recall on every prompt or weight change. |

Several things are deliberately left out. Skip Demucs and pyannote on the whole VOD (each costs close to real time on CPU), and do not trust Whisper laughter tags. Whisper-AT does not fit faster-whisper. Skip CLIP prompts for "funny", scene-cut density on streams, game OCR, and local Qwen3-VL on CPU. Facecam blendshapes come last, if evaluation shows that silent reactions are being missed. One policy point: reading the heatmap through yt-dlp uses the same unofficial mechanism Spike already relies on for downloads, not browser automation. It remains outside YouTube's official API and can break without notice, so treat it as optional. Comments should go through the official Data API.

## Conclusion

The research changes which part of Spike should do the finding. The LLM is the most expensive and least reliable humor judge in the stack, while laughter and crowd replays are cheap and directly encode human reaction. That inverts the current upload path, where the LLM proposes and loudness only breaks ties. Signals should propose, and the LLM should judge a short, setup-inclusive window, with non-verbal context written into its prompt. The biggest uncertainty is whether a streamer's own laughter predicts good clips as well as audience laughter does in the datasets. A 10-VOD evaluation answers that, and it decides the laugh weight and the 0.7/0.3 split at the same time.

The review screen matters more than any single model choice. Every accept and reject is a labeled window, the exact training signal that Mr. HiSum and the humor datasets had to manufacture. Logging features per candidate from day one costs nothing and turns the hand-tuned weights in section 6.1 into measured ones within a few weeks of use. No commercial or open-source clipper surveyed publishes that feedback loop.
